# Model Compression & Brand Recall: What Survives Distillation and Quantization

## Key Insight
LLM compression creates a brand survival hierarchy: (1) Distillation transfers knowledge from teacher to student model, but trades recall for precision, concentrating probability on high-frequency entities while losing tail coverage (Source). (2) Quantization (FP16 to INT4/INT2) preserves 95-99% of benchmark accuracy but degrades first-hop factual reasoning by up to 30%, with unsaturated knowledge relations most vulnerable (Source). (3) On-device deployment (Apple 3B at 2-bit, Gemini Nano, Phi-3) forces extreme compression where only dominant brand associations survive (Source, Source). (4) Model collapse from synthetic training data erases minority content first (Source). The combined effect: brands with lower training data frequency are disproportionately erased at each compression stage, creating winner-take-all dynamics in AI recommendations.

### Knowledge Distillation: What Transfers, What Dies (patent)
- Teacher-student transfer: Distillation trains small models to mimic larger ones' output distributions (Source). Gemma 2 (2B/9B) distilled from Gemini shows up to 10% benchmark improvement over previous versions (Source). But distillation induces a precision-recall tradeoff: student models concentrate probability mass on high-likelihood regions while sacrificing tail coverage
- Phi-4 beyond distillation: Microsoft's Phi-4 (14B) trained on synthetic data from GPT-4 surpasses its teacher on STEM benchmarks (GPQA, MATH) (Source). Uses only 3% of original training data for retraining. Proves synthetic data pipelines can exceed teacher capabilities in narrow domains while potentially losing breadth
- NVIDIA Minitron: Combines structured pruning + distillation to compress Nemotron-4 15B to 8B and 4B (Source). Achieves 16% MMLU improvement and 40x reduction in training tokens. Uses KL divergence loss exclusively (no conventional training loss), forcing student to match teacher's full probability distribution
- What gets lost: Emergent abilities (chain-of-thought reasoning) require sophisticated transfer techniques (Source). Multilingual and multimodal capabilities often dropped in smaller distilled models (Gemma 1/2 lost Gemini's multilingual support) (Source). Low-frequency entity associations are the first casualty of distribution compression

### Quantization: Precision Loss and Entity Memory (leak)
- Factual Knowledge Recall (FKR) study: Testing Llama3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B/14B across GPTQ, AWQ, BitsAndBytes at 4-bit and 8-bit (Source). Accuracy drops 1.3-6.2 pp for Llama3-8B, 2.5-3.2 pp for Qwen2.5-7B. Critically: first-hop reasoning degrades up to 30.08% while second-hop only 4.25%, meaning the initial entity recall step is most vulnerable
- Unsaturated knowledge is most fragile: Relations where full-precision performance hasn't saturated show more severe FKR degradation (Source). This means less-established facts (niche brand associations, emerging entities) are disproportionately damaged by quantization
- Red Hat mega-study: 500,000+ evaluations on Llama 3.1 (8B/70B/405B) (Source). 8-bit recovers 99%+ accuracy. 4-bit recovers 98.9% on HumanEval. But 8B models show 'more variability in word selection' than larger models, meaning brand name substitution is more likely in small quantized models
- 2-bit catastrophic failure: 2-bit integer quantization causes complete breakdown in coherent response generation (Source). Repetitive out-of-context tokens. Model's next-token prediction collapses. This is the precision floor for Apple Intelligence's 2-bit on-device model
- Confidence shifts: Quantized LLaMA-2-7B shows 13% confidence reduction on toxicity tasks, 11% on commonsense reasoning (Source). Lower confidence = more randomness in entity selection = less consistent brand recommendations

### Quantization Formats: GGUF, GPTQ, AWQ (exploit)
- GGUF quality tiers: Q8_0 retains 99.5% quality (8.5 GB/8B model). Q6_K retains 98% (6.7 GB, production sweet spot). Q4_K_M retains 95% (4.7 GB). IQ2_M retains only 85% (2.5 GB). Non-English languages need Q6_K minimum for 98-99% quality preservation (Source)
- AWQ vs GPTQ: AWQ achieves 96% quality retention by protecting the critical 1% of weights based on activation magnitudes. GPTQ achieves 95-96% using second-order derivatives. AWQ preserves coherence better for creative/recommendation workloads. GPTQ wins raw CUDA throughput (Source)
- EXL2 per-layer allocation: Configurable 2.5-6 bits per layer. Best-in-class at any target size but limited to ExLlama v2 runtime. Allows protecting knowledge-critical layers while aggressively compressing less important ones (Source)
- Speed vs quality tradeoff: Q4_K_M GGUF: 190 tok/s at 95% quality. Q6_K GGUF: 161 tok/s at 98% quality (Source). The 3% quality gap between 4-bit and 6-bit represents the margin where tail entity recall degrades
- Apple's approach: 2-bit QAT for decoder weights, 4-bit for embeddings, 8-bit for KV cache (Source). Mixed precision preserves entity embeddings (4-bit) while aggressively compressing generation layers (2-bit). Competitive with Qwen-2.5-3B and Gemma-3-4B despite smaller effective precision

### On-Device AI: The Brand Knowledge Bottleneck (patent)
- Market scale: 370 million GenAI smartphones shipped in 2025, surging to 912 million by 2028 (290% growth) (Source). 30%+ of shipped smartphones are GenAI-enabled by end of 2025. Samsung Galaxy AI projected at 400 million devices by end 2025. On-device AI market: $10.8B in 2025, projected $75.5B by 2033 (Source)
- Apple Intelligence 3B model: Explicitly 'not designed to be a chatbot for general world knowledge.' Excels at summarization, entity extraction, text understanding, short dialog (Source). Brand/product knowledge limited to Apple's own ecosystem. For general knowledge queries, routes to server-side models or ChatGPT integration
- Qualcomm NPU capacity: Snapdragon 8 Elite features 80+ TOPS NPU. Enables Llama 3.2 1B/3B and Phi-3 Mini to run entirely on-device (Source). But 1-3B parameter models store vastly fewer entity associations than 70B+ cloud models
- The hybrid routing problem: On-device models handle simple queries locally; complex/knowledge queries route to cloud. Brand visibility depends on which path handles the query. If a recommendation query stays on-device, only brands surviving extreme compression get mentioned

### Model Collapse and Synthetic Data: Tail Erasure (leak)
- Nature 2024 study: Training on recursively generated AI data causes irreversible defects (Source). Tails of the original distribution disappear first. Early collapse: minority data vanishes while overall performance appears stable. Late collapse: model converges to low-variance point estimate
- Web content contamination: 74.2% of newly created webpages contain AI-generated text (April 2025). AI-written pages in Google's top-20 climbed from 11.1% to 19.6% between May 2024 and July 2025 (Source). Future training data increasingly synthetic, accelerating tail erosion
- Niche brand implications: Model collapse erases minority content first, meaning brands with lower web frequency are progressively forgotten across model generations (Source). The effect is 'particularly insidious because overall performance may appear to improve' while minority data disappears
- Mitigation exists but is imperfect: Accumulating synthetic data alongside original real data prevents collapse (Source). But this requires deliberate curation. Scraping the internet indiscriminately (the default approach) guarantees progressive tail loss. Grammar checkers and LLM-as-judge pipelines filter quality but not diversity

### Model Merging: Knowledge Recombination (exploit)
- MergeKit ecosystem: Thousands of merged models created by open-source community (Source). Some reach top rankings on Open LLM Leaderboard. Merging combines parameters of models with different capabilities without additional training
- TIES-Merging: Resets minimal parameter changes, resolves sign conflicts, merges only aligned parameters (Source). Preserves pre-trained capabilities while integrating new knowledge. Addresses catastrophic forgetting during merge
- Knowledge fragility in merging: Fine-tuned models can significantly deviate from base model, causing performance degradation analogous to catastrophic forgetting (Source). Activation-informed approaches preserve pre-trained capabilities better. Brand knowledge from the base model can be overwritten by fine-tuned specializations
- Evolutionary merging: Nature Machine Intelligence published evolutionary optimization of merge recipes (Source). Automated search for optimal merge configurations. Can potentially recover knowledge lost in individual compression steps

### Calculations
- Brand Survival Through Compression: A brand's probability of surviving model compression depends on its training frequency, the compression method, and the bit-width. High-frequency brands (appearing in thousands of training documents) maintain stable token probabilities through quantization. Low-frequency brands (appearing in tens of documents) fall below the noise floor at aggressive quantization levels, effectively disappearing from model outputs. Affects: Determines whether a brand is mentioned by on-device and quantized models. Brands below a frequency threshold are effectively erased at each compression stage.
- On-Device Brand Reach: The number of users whose brand queries are answered by compressed on-device models rather than full-precision cloud models. As GenAI smartphone adoption grows (370M in 2025, 912M by 2028), an increasing share of brand-related queries are handled by 1-3B parameter models at 2-4 bit precision, where only high-frequency brands have reliable recall. Affects: Quantifies the growing audience receiving brand recommendations from compressed models. Niche brands face increasing invisibility as on-device AI scales.
- Model Collapse Tail Erosion Rate: Each generation of models trained on increasingly AI-generated web data loses tail distribution coverage. With 74.2% of new webpages containing AI-generated text, future training corpora are progressively contaminated. Brands in the distribution tail (low frequency) are erased first, while head brands are reinforced. The effect compounds across model generations. Affects: Predicts progressive disappearance of niche brands from LLM training data across model generations. Creates compounding advantage for already-dominant brands.
- Quantization Quality Ladder: Each quantization level trades model quality for efficiency. The quality retention percentages map to specific brand recall implications: at 99.5% (Q8), nearly all entity associations are preserved; at 95% (Q4), tail entities begin degrading; at 85% (Q2/IQ2_M), only high-frequency entities maintain stable recall. Apple's mixed-precision approach (2-bit weights, 4-bit embeddings, 8-bit KV cache) optimizes for entity recognition while minimizing generation precision. Affects: Maps compression levels to expected brand recall degradation. Guides understanding of which deployment targets (cloud Q8, edge Q4, mobile Q2) preserve brand visibility.

### Brand Knowledge Compression Pipeline
- Pre-training: Brand associations learned from Common Crawl, Wikipedia, Reddit, books. Frequency and context determine association strength. High-frequency brands encoded as strong token probability peaks. Tail brands encoded as weak, noisy signals.
- Knowledge Distillation: Teacher model (Gemini, GPT-4) transfers knowledge to student (Gemma, Phi) (Source). Precision-recall tradeoff: student concentrates on high-probability outputs. Tail brand associations lost as distribution is compressed. Only 3% of training data needed for retraining (Minitron).
- Pruning: Structured removal of neurons, attention heads, or layers. NVIDIA Minitron prunes 15B to 8B/4B (Source). Redundant representations eliminated. Brand knowledge stored in pruned components is permanently lost (Source).
- Quantization: Weights reduced from FP16/BF16 to INT8/INT4/INT2. First-hop entity recall degrades up to 30% (Source). Unsaturated knowledge relations most vulnerable. Apple uses mixed precision: 2-bit weights, 4-bit embeddings, 8-bit KV cache (Source).
- Format Export: Model exported to GGUF, GPTQ, AWQ, or proprietary format (Source). AWQ preserves critical 1% of weights for coherence. GGUF Q4_K_M at 95% quality is the common deployment target. EXL2 allows per-layer bit allocation for knowledge preservation.
- On-Device Deployment: 1-3B model runs on Snapdragon NPU (80+ TOPS), Apple A19 Pro, or similar (Source). Brand queries either answered locally (compressed model) or routed to cloud (full model). Routing decision determines whether compressed or full brand knowledge is used.
- Model Collapse Feedback: Model outputs become training data for next generation. 74.2% of new web content is AI-generated (Source). Tail distributions erode across generations (Source). Dominant brands reinforced while niche brands progressively forgotten.

### Contradictions
- Public (Model benchmarks and marketing): Quantized models retain 95-99% of full-precision accuracy. Compression is essentially lossless for practical purposes. vs Internal (FKR research (<a href="https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13963">Source</a>): first-hop recall degrades 30%, tail entities disproportionately affected): Aggregate benchmarks mask asymmetric degradation. While average accuracy drops only 1-5%, first-hop factual recall (the initial entity retrieval step) degrades up to 30% (Source). Knowledge relations that haven't saturated in training show the worst degradation. Niche brand associations, being unsaturated by definition, are disproportionately erased. The 95% quality retention headline hides a long tail of missing entities.
- Public (On-device AI marketing (Apple, Google, Samsung)): On-device AI delivers powerful, intelligent assistance while protecting user privacy. Same capabilities, no cloud needed. vs Internal (Apple ML Research (<a href="https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/apple-foundation-models-2025-updates">Source</a>): 3B model 'not designed for general world knowledge'): On-device models (1-3B parameters at 2-4 bit precision) are explicitly not general knowledge systems (Source). They handle summarization, entity extraction, and short dialog, but lack the parameter capacity to store broad brand/entity associations. Complex queries route to cloud models, but the routing decision is opaque to users and brands. A brand recommendation query handled on-device draws from a drastically compressed knowledge base.
- Public (Synthetic data optimists): Synthetic data enables better, cheaper training. Phi-4 surpasses GPT-4 on STEM using synthetic data (Source). Model collapse is overhyped. vs Internal (Nature 2024 (<a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y">Source</a>): tail erasure is irreversible; 74% of web content now AI-generated (<a href="https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/2025-ai-citation-llm-visibility-report/">Source</a>)): Synthetic data excels in narrow, well-defined domains (math, code) but systematically erases distribution tails (Source). Phi-4 surpasses GPT-4 on STEM precisely because STEM has well-defined answers, but this says nothing about broad entity knowledge. With 74.2% of new web content AI-generated, future training corpora will contain fewer original mentions of niche brands, compounding tail erasure across model generations.

### Overview
When LLMs are compressed via knowledge distillation (Gemma from Gemini, Phi from GPT-4) or quantization (FP16 to INT4), brand and entity recall degrades asymmetrically. Tail entities lose up to 30% factual accuracy (Source) while dominant brands retain 99%+ recall (Source). Apple Intelligence runs a 3B-parameter model at 2-bit quantization (Source). 370 million GenAI smartphones shipped in 2025, reaching 912 million by 2028 (Source). On-device models are explicitly 'not designed for general world knowledge,' meaning brand associations must survive extreme compression or vanish entirely. Model collapse from synthetic training data compounds the problem: tails of the distribution disappear first, erasing niche brands from AI memory (Source). The compression pipeline (distillation, pruning, quantization, merging) creates a brand visibility hierarchy where only the most frequently referenced entities survive intact.
