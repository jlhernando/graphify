# Key People & Organizations: The Transformer Diaspora

## Key Insight
The eight Transformer co-authors dispersed from Google to build competing AI companies, creating a unique situation: the inventors of the foundational architecture now lead rival organizations training models on each other's research. Noam Shazeer returned to Google (via Character.AI acquisition) to lead Gemini. Lukasz Kaiser leads GPT-4/5 development at OpenAI. Aidan Gomez runs Cohere ($5.5B). The remaining five founded startups across AI, biotech, crypto, and nature-inspired computing. Meanwhile, Dario Amodei (ex-OpenAI VP Research) and Ilya Sutskever (ex-OpenAI Chief Scientist) each founded safety-focused competitors. The talent graph is the org chart of AI.

### The Eight Transformer Authors (2017) (patent)
- Ashish Vaswani (first author): Co-founder & CEO of Essential AI. Left Google to build enterprise AI infrastructure. Led the core Transformer design including multi-head self-attention
- Noam Shazeer: Co-founded Character.AI (2021), acquired by Google for $2.7B (Aug 2024). Now leads Gemini development at Google DeepMind. Also co-invented Switch Transformer (Mixture of Experts). Most commercially impactful of the eight
- Niki Parmar: Joined Anthropic (Dec 2024) after stints at Adept AI and Essential AI. Contributed to Claude 3.7 Sonnet development
- Jakob Uszkoreit: Co-founder & CEO of Inceptive. Pivoted from AI to biotech, applying ML to RNA drug design
- Llion Jones: Co-founder & CTO of Sakana AI (Tokyo). Focus on nature-inspired AI and evolutionary algorithms
- Aidan Gomez: Co-founder & CEO of Cohere, valued at $5.5B. Was an intern during the Transformer paper. Now competes directly with Google in enterprise AI
- Lukasz Kaiser: Member of Technical Staff at OpenAI. Led core R&D for GPT-4, GPT-5, o1, and o3. Directly building on the architecture he co-invented
- Illia Polosukhin: Co-founder of NEAR Protocol, a decentralized AI and blockchain platform. Left Google in 2017 immediately after the paper

### AI Lab Leaders & Power Brokers (leak)
- Jeff Dean (Google Chief Scientist): Designed MapReduce, BigTable, Spanner, TensorFlow, TPUs, BERT, PaLM, Gemini. 200+ papers. Most impactful individual engineer at Google. Controls the infrastructure Transformer models run on
- Dario Amodei (Anthropic CEO): Former VP Research at OpenAI, led GPT-2 and GPT-3 development. Left over safety disagreements. PhD in computational neuroscience from Princeton. Anthropic valued at $60B+
- Daniela Amodei (Anthropic President): Former VP Operations at OpenAI. Led Anthropic fundraising ($7.6B+). Background at Stripe and US Congress. Manages business operations while Dario leads research
- Ilya Sutskever: Co-founder and Chief Scientist of OpenAI (2015-2024). Co-authored AlexNet (2012). Left June 2024, co-founded Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), valued at $5B with $1B raised. Pivoted entirely to alignment research
- Andrej Karpathy: Built GPT training infrastructure at OpenAI. Former Director of AI at Tesla (Autopilot). Created nanoGPT, minbpe, llm.c. Founded Eureka Labs (2024) for AI education. PhD from Stanford under Fei-Fei Li. Most influential AI educator
- Pandu Nayak (Google VP Search): Testified extensively during DOJ antitrust trial on RankBrain, DeepRank, MUM, and quality metrics. Key figure connecting Transformer research to Google Search ranking systems

### Talent Movements & Acquisitions (doj)
- Character.AI to Google ($2.7B, Aug 2024): Google reacquired Noam Shazeer via licensing deal. Shazeer now leads Gemini. Pattern: Google loses Transformer talent, then buys companies to get them back
- OpenAI exodus to Anthropic (2021): Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, and ~10 researchers left over safety concerns and commercialization direction. Took GPT-scaling expertise to build Claude
- Sutskever's departure (Jun 2024): After the board crisis (Nov 2023) and Sam Altman's reinstatement, Sutskever left to found SSI. Signal: even OpenAI's chief scientist felt alignment was being deprioritized
- Meta's open-source strategy: Hugo Touvron led LLaMA development, releasing open weights. Created the open-source LLM ecosystem (Llama 2, Llama 3). Forced competitive pressure on closed-source labs
- DeepSeek emergence (Hangzhou): Released V3 and R1 models competitive with frontier Western labs. Demonstrated that Transformer scaling works globally, not just in US/UK labs
- Mistral (Paris, founded May 2023): Ex-Meta and DeepMind researchers. European alternative to US-dominated AI labs. Rapidly reached competitive model quality

### Organization Map & Strategic Implications (exploit)
- Frontier model labs: OpenAI (GPT), Anthropic (Claude), Google DeepMind (Gemini), Meta (Llama), DeepSeek (V3/R1), Mistral. Each shapes which brands, sources, and knowledge get embedded in their models' training data
- Open-source ecosystem: EleutherAI (The Pile dataset), AI2/Allen Institute (OLMo), Alibaba DAMO (Qwen), TII Abu Dhabi (Falcon). These orgs determine what enters the open training data commons
- Infrastructure & tooling: HuggingFace (model hosting, TRL framework), Scale AI and Surge AI (human labeling for SFT/RLHF), Pinecone and Weaviate (vector DBs), LangChain and LlamaIndex (RAG frameworks). The picks-and-shovels layer
- RAG-first search: Perplexity (RAG-based search), Google AI Overviews, Bing Chat/Copilot. These systems determine which sources get cited in AI-generated answers. Traditional SEO signals feed RAG retrieval
- Key implication for SEO: Brand visibility now depends on decisions made by ~50 people across 6-8 organizations. Training data curation, RLHF preferences, and RAG retrieval logic are controlled by a small talent pool, many of whom worked together at Google or OpenAI

### Transformer Talent Flow Pipeline
- Google Brain (2017): Eight researchers publish 'Attention Is All You Need.' All employed at Google. Architecture initially applied to machine translation.
- Internal Application (2018-2020): Google applies Transformers to Search: BERT (2018), MUM (2019), DeepRank. RankBrain team (Strohmann, Nayak) integrates neural ranking. OpenAI scales GPT.
- Diaspora Begins (2020-2022): Authors leave Google: Polosukhin (NEAR), Gomez (Cohere), Shazeer (Character.AI), Uszkoreit (Inceptive). Amodei siblings leave OpenAI for Anthropic (2021).
- Competition Intensifies (2023-2024): ChatGPT launch (Nov 2022) triggers AI race. Mistral founded. Meta releases LLaMA. DeepSeek emerges. Sutskever leaves OpenAI for SSI. Kaiser leads GPT-4/5.
- Reconsolidation (2024-2025): Google reacquires Shazeer via Character.AI deal ($2.7B). Parmar joins Anthropic. Talent clusters reform around 6-8 frontier labs. Training data and RLHF decisions concentrated among ~18 people.
- SEO Implications (2025+): Brand visibility depends on AI model outputs. The small talent pool controlling model training, fine-tuning, and RAG design shapes which brands get recommended. Traditional SEO gives way to GEO (Generative Engine Optimization).

### Timeline
- 2012 (event): AlexNet - Sutskever co-authors AlexNet with Krizhevsky and Hinton, launching the deep learning era that makes Transformers possible.
- 2017 (event): Attention Is All You Need - Vaswani, Shazeer, Parmar, Uszkoreit, Jones, Gomez, Kaiser, and Polosukhin publish the Transformer paper at Google Brain. Cited 130,000+ times.
- 2018 (event): BERT & GPT-1 - Google applies Transformers to Search via BERT. OpenAI releases GPT-1. The Transformer becomes the dominant architecture for NLP.
- 2020 (event): GPT-3 & Scaling Laws - OpenAI releases GPT-3 (175B params). Kaplan et al. publish scaling laws. Dario Amodei leads the effort as VP Research.
- 2021 (event): Anthropic Founded & Character.AI - Dario and Daniela Amodei leave OpenAI, found Anthropic. Shazeer co-founds Character.AI. The exodus from Google and OpenAI accelerates.
- 2022 (event): ChatGPT Launch - OpenAI releases ChatGPT (Nov 30). Fastest-growing consumer app ever. Triggers the AI arms race among all frontier labs.
- 2023 (event): Open-Source Explosion - Meta releases LLaMA/Llama 2. Mistral founded (Paris). DeepSeek begins competing from China. AI lab count multiplies globally.
- 2024 (event): Reconsolidation & DOJ Trial - Google acquires Character.AI ($2.7B), gets Shazeer back for Gemini. Sutskever leaves OpenAI, founds SSI. Parmar joins Anthropic. DOJ antitrust trial exposes Google Search internals.
- 2025 (event): GEO Era Begins - AI Overviews, Perplexity, and ChatGPT Search reshape brand visibility. ~18 people across 8 orgs control which brands get recommended. SEO evolves into GEO.

### Contradictions
- Public (Google): We democratize AI through open research and publications. vs Internal (Talent acquisitions): Google spent $2.7B to reacquire a single Transformer co-author (Shazeer) via Character.AI deal, concentrating rather than distributing AI talent.
- Public (OpenAI): Our mission is to ensure AGI benefits all of humanity. vs Internal (Researcher departures): Chief Scientist Sutskever and VP Research Amodei both left citing safety and commercialization concerns, founding competing safety-focused organizations.
- Public (AI Industry): AI development is broadly distributed across many organizations globally. vs Internal (Talent analysis): Approximately 18 people with shared Google Brain or OpenAI lineage control frontier model development at organizations representing >80% of AI compute.
- Public (Meta): Open-source AI benefits the entire ecosystem equally. vs Internal (Strategic analysis): Open-sourcing LLaMA was primarily a competitive strategy to commoditize the model layer where rivals (OpenAI, Anthropic) have advantages, while Meta retains advantages in distribution (3B+ users).

### Overview
Eight researchers authored 'Attention Is All You Need' (2017) at Google Brain. By 2026, all eight have left Google to found or lead competing AI companies collectively valued at over $20B. This diaspora created the organizational landscape of modern AI: Cohere ($5.5B), Character.AI (acquired by Google for $2.7B), Essential AI, Sakana AI, Inceptive, and NEAR Protocol. Meanwhile, former OpenAI leaders founded Anthropic ($60B+) and Safe Superintelligence ($5B). Understanding who built what, where they went, and what they control reveals the power structure behind the models that increasingly mediate search, recommendations, and brand visibility.
