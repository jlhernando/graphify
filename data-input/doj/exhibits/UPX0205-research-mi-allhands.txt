A simple but powerful algorithm for "the choice problem" is the "Priors algorithm".

The idea is to score the doors based on how many people took it.
In other words, you rank the choices based on how popular it is.
This is simple, yet very powerful: it is one of the strongest signals for much or Google's search and ads ranking !
If we know nothing about the user, this is probably the best thing we can do.

We bring two twists to the traditional heuristic.
Instead of attempting to describe -- through a noisy process -- what each door is about, we describe it based on the
people who took it
We can do this at Google, because at our scale, even the most obscure choice would have been exercised by thousands
of people.
When a new user walks in , we measure their similarity to the people behind each door.
This brings us to the second twist, which is that
while describing a user, we dont not use demographics or other stereotypical attributes.
We simply use a user's past actions to describe them and match users based on their behavioral similarity.
